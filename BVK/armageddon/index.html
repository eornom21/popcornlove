Fast facts

  • Director Michael Bay · Producers Jerry Bruckheimer, Gale Anne Hurd, Michael Bay
  • Screenplay Jonathan Hensleigh & J.J. Abrams · Adaptation Tony Gilroy & Shane Salerno · Story Robert Roy Pool & Hensleigh. Uncredited passes by Paul Attanasio, Ann Biderman, Scott Rosenberg and Robert Towne (Chinatown). Nine writers on a movie about drilling a hole.
  • Box office $201.6M domestic, $352.1M overseas. Biggest movie in the world in 1998; second in the US behind Saving Private Ryan.
  • Shot Aug 27, 1997 – Jan 29, 1998, mostly at Culver Studios. Premiered at Kennedy Space Center June 29, 1998.
  • Narrated by Charlton Heston.
  • The rival Deep Impact (Mimi Leder) opened May 8, 1998, seven weeks earlier, on a $75M budget — and made $349M. Deep Impact's writer says a Disney exec took notes on their script over lunch and Armageddon was the answer. Disney's Joe Roth added $3M in May '98 just to blow up Paris so the two movies wouldn't look alike.

Scoreboard

  • Rotten Tomatoes 42% critics · 73% audience. Consensus: pretty, dumb, exactly what it says on the tin.
  • Ebert One star. Called it an assault on "the eyes, the ears, the brain, common sense."
  • Oscars Four nominations, zero wins: Sound, Sound Effects Editing, Visual Effects, Original Song.
  • Razzies Bruce Willis won Worst Actor (shared credit with Mercury Rising and The Siege). Also nominated for Worst Picture, Director, Screenplay, Supporting Actress (Tyler), Screen Couple (Affleck/Tyler) and Worst Original Song — the same song the Oscars nominated.
  • Saturn Awards Won Best Science Fiction Film and Best Director. Same year, same movie, same Michael Bay.
  • MTV Movie Awards Best Action Sequence — the asteroid shower flattening Manhattan.
  • Criterion Collection Spine #40. A director's cut with a Jeanine Basinger essay defending it. Still the single most argued-about title in the collection.

Drinking with the Director

Michael Bay

  • Born Feb 17, 1965, Los Angeles. Wesleyan, class of '86, English and film. Then Art Center in Pasadena.
  • Cut his teeth on commercials and videos: the 1993 Got Milk? "Aaron Burr" spot (Clio Grand Prix, Commercial of the Year) and a 1992 Red Cross spot that won a Clio.
  • Features: Bad Boys (1995), The Rock (1996), Armageddon (1998), Pearl Harbor (2001), Bad Boys II (2003), The Island (2005), Transformers (2007, 2009, 2011), Pain & Gain (2013). This was movie three.
  • Over $6.6 billion worldwide as a director — fifth-most commercially successful ever.
  • His own line on the whole thing: "I make movies for teenage boys. Oh, dear, what a crime."
  • Suggested pour: whatever it is, it should be loud, orange, and served in slow motion.

The song

"I Don't Want to Miss a Thing"

  • Written by Diane Warren, who pictured "Celine Dion or somebody like that" singing it. U2 were approached first.
  • Went to Aerosmith after Liv Tyler was cast — her dad's band, for her movie.
  • Hit #1 on the Hot 100 on Sept 5, 1998 and stayed four weeks. Aerosmith's first and only #1 in the US, twenty-five years into the band.
  • Oscar-nominated for Best Original Song. Razzie-nominated for Worst Original Song. Won the MTV Video Music Award for Best Video from a Film.

The science fight

  • The asteroid is "the size of Texas." The largest known potentially hazardous asteroid is about 7 km across.
  • In 2011 a group of Leicester physics postgrads worked out that splitting it Harry's way would take an H-bomb about a billion times stronger than Tsar Bomba, the biggest ever detonated.
  • Neil deGrasse Tyson said it broke more laws of physics per minute than any movie he'd seen — a title it held until Moonfall (2022).
  • NASA cooperated on the film and then put a disclaimer in the credits saying cooperation was not endorsement.
  • Bay has admitted the central premise — that NASA would need oil drillers — is nonsense. Ben Affleck asked him on the DVD commentary why it was easier to train drillers to be astronauts than astronauts to drill; Bay told him to shut up.
  • The legend: NASA supposedly screens this in management training and asks trainees to find the 168 scientific errors. It's everywhere online and nobody has ever produced the NASA document. Treat it as folklore — or as a draft.

Trivia that plays on air

  • Super Bowl XXXII spot cost $2.6 million. Billboards were realistic enough to cause some local panic.
  • After wrap, Richard Karn from Home Improvement found the asteroid set. Tim Allen asked Bay to borrow it; Bay said yes if they waited six months. It became a credits gag where Wilson's basement caves in.
  • VHS and DVD landed Nov 13, 1998 and passed Pretty Woman as Buena Vista's best-selling live-action title. In the UK it sold 100,000 DVDs — a record until The Matrix.
  • After 9/11, ABC cut the shot of the meteor hitting the World Trade Center. After the 2003 Columbia disaster, FX pulled the movie from its schedule and ran Aliens instead.
  • Disneyland Paris opened Armageddon – Les Effets Spéciaux in March 2002: you stand inside the Mir set while it comes apart, with Michael Clarke Duncan hosting the pre-show. It ran until 2019.
  • Eddie Griffin is the bike messenger. Udo Kier is the psychologist. Shawnee Smith (Saw) is the redhead.
